# Taste

Impeccable asks whether it was built properly. This asks whether it should exist
in that form at all.

Taste is mostly subtraction. The common failure in this project's kind of work —
a boutique storefront — is not ugliness. It's a page that is busy, evenly weighted,
and says nothing in particular.

## 1. One thing leads

Every screen has a single job. Name it in a sentence before designing:
*"This page makes someone add a product to the cart."*

Then rank everything on the screen against that job. Exactly one element wins —
in size, weight, or colour. If three things compete for first place, the reader
picks none of them.

- The lead element earns its emphasis; everything else steps back.
- Two competing calls-to-action is one too many. Choose the primary, demote the other
  to a quiet link.
- Emphasis is a budget. Bold everything and you've bolded nothing.

## 2. Subtract first

Before adding a section, a badge, a divider, or an icon, ask what it does for the
reader. Decoration that carries no information is cost with no return.

- Cut copy that restates the obvious. A product tile does not need the word "Product".
- Cut a divider that separates things whitespace already separates.
- Cut an icon that duplicates its adjacent label.
- Structural devices — numbering, eyebrows, rules — must encode something *true*.
  Number a list only when order genuinely matters. Numbering an unordered set is a lie
  about the content.

If removing something doesn't hurt, it was decoration.

## 3. Typography carries more than colour does

- Set a type scale and stay on it. A new arbitrary size is how a design starts to drift.
- Running text sits near 65 characters. Much wider and the eye loses its place.
- Hierarchy comes from size, weight, and space — reach for those before reaching for colour.
- Uppercase labels need letter-spacing; body text does not.
- Headings get `text-wrap: balance`. A heading breaking to one orphaned word looks careless.
- Numbers that line up in columns get `tabular-nums`.

## 4. Colour with discipline

- Neutrals are chosen, not inherited. A pure mid-grey reads as unconsidered; a neutral
  carrying a slight bias toward the brand hue reads as deliberate.
- Accent colour is a scarce resource. It marks what to act on. When it decorates,
  it stops meaning anything.
- Semantic colour — success, warning, danger — is separate from the brand accent
  and never doubles as it.
- Both themes get real design, not a naive inversion. An accent that sings on cream
  often goes muddy on charcoal and needs lifting.

## 5. Copy is design material

Words are on the screen, so they are part of the design.

- Name things the way a customer would. "Delivery", not "fulfilment status".
- A control states what happens: **Publish** → toast reads *Published*.
- Specific beats clever. "Ships in 2 days" outperforms "Lightning fast!".
- Write from the reader's side of the screen, in active voice.

## 6. Claims must be true

This is a real shop selling real goods, so this section is not stylistic.

- Never invent social proof. No fabricated review counts, ratings, testimonials,
  or "1,284 people bought this". A rating shown beside zero reviews is a false claim.
- Never invent scarcity or urgency that isn't real — no fake countdowns, no
  "only 2 left" unless stock genuinely says 2.
- Product claims match what the manufacturer actually states. A reseller does not
  inherit the right to call a mixed catalogue "clean", "vegan", or "sustainably sourced".
- When there's no data yet, design the honest empty state instead of filling it with fiction.
  It converts worse for a week and survives contact with customers.

## 7. Avoid the generic default

Recognisable tells of undesigned design: everything centred; uniform card grids with
no size variation; an accent bar on every rounded card; emoji as section markers;
evenly-weighted sections with no lead; gradient hero on white; stock phrases like
"Elevate your routine".

Where the brand already has a direction, follow it. Where nothing is specified,
don't spend the freedom on the nearest default.

## The check

1. What is this screen's one job, and does the design say so in the first second?
2. What did I remove?
3. Is every emphasised thing worth the emphasis?
4. Is every claim on this page actually true?
